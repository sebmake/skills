# Grammar of the voiceless product film

This file explains how to build a product film in the manner of keynote reveal films when there is **no voice**. It covers the product film (60 to 120 s). The social cut-downs have their rules in `declinaisons.md`, the shots in `recettes.md`, and the `film.json` format in `film-spec.md`.

## 1. How to read this file

The corpus: 8 keynotes, from 2019 to 2026. The counts use five bases. One film can belong to several.

| Base | What it is | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 20 product films | reveal (8), materials (4), software (6), use (2), trimmed to the frame | durations, shots, structure, name placement |
| 14 object films | reveal films of an object, viewed at 2 frames per second | camera, light, color, transitions |
| 12 interface films | films that show a screen | screens, moves, text placed next to a screen |
| 33 texts | sentences and names added in the edit, across 48 films and openers | text size, placement, motion |
| 109 cards | cards read in 51 films (excluding name bars and logos) | how long a card stays, by word count |

Labels:

- **observed N/M**: seen in N cases out of the M in the base;
- **convention**: not observed, or too rarely. It is a choice of the skill. Apply it as is.

**The central fact.** In keynotes, the film shows and the speaker explains. 15 product films out of 20 have no voice, and 8 reveal films out of 8. But a speaker talks just before and just after. Here there is no voice-over and no speaker. On-screen text takes over, using the grammar of the films: few words, placed calmly, at the right moment. This is the skill's main adaptation. It is marked convention wherever it departs from what was observed.

## 2. The five beats of a film

Observed order: **hook → build-up → reveal → name → end**, in 13 product films out of the 14 where the reveal can be located. The exception shows the object at 9% of the film, then its features, and ends on a lineup shot.

| Beat | What happens | Observed duration, median (min–max) | Share of duration, median | Present in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | a detail, a material, a glow coming out of black; never the whole object | 5.6 s (0.6–26.6) | 14.5% | 20/20 |
| Build-up | features, components, uses, in short shots | 21.6 s (13.4–90.8) | 67% | 20/20 |
| Reveal | the first shot of the whole object (or all the screens), held | 7.8 s (0.9–12.8) | 16% | 14/20 |
| Name | the name on a card | 3.5 s (1.3–16.3) | 8% | 10/20 write it |
| End | black, logo, color lineup, or a held last shot | 4.9 s (1.0–7.0) | 5% | 5/20 have a distinct end |

### 2.1 The hook

- One simple sign, observed in 9 object films out of 14: a chip lighting up (3), a rim of light on black (2), a full-frame color pattern (2), a raw material (2).
- Shots of 2.5 to 4 s (median 2.8 s, 56 shots measured).
- 9 films out of 51 start on black. For the product film: 0.5 to 1 s of black, then one element lights up over 1.2 to 3 s (convention, close to 3 interface films out of 12 that come out of black).
- Without a voice, a thesis card can close the hook (convention; a thesis sentence opens 4 of the 11 films with message cards).

### 2.2 The build-up

- Shots of 0.7 to 2 s (median 1.17 s; quartiles 0.67 and 2.10 s; 662 shots).
- The object is shown in pieces: macro (12/14 object films), exploded view (6/14), streak of light (4/14).
- Software is shown one feature at a time: one interface subject per shot, about 3.3 s each (observed in one feature film, 10 subjects in 33 s).
- Past 60 s, the build-up splits into chapters (§ 3).

### 2.3 The reveal

- It starts between 63 and 94% of the duration (median 76%) in 13 product films out of 14; in the last third for 11 out of 14.
- One long shot: median 5.5 s (quartiles 2.7 and 8.3 s; 17 shots).
- The last shot before black shows the whole object, alone, centered, still or turning slowly: observed 10/14.
- A camera pull-back often goes with it: observed 6/14.
- Another pattern, observed 5/14: the whole object from the start (before 30%), then viewpoints and uses. It suits a use film or a color lineup. A film follows only one of the two patterns (convention).

### 2.4 The name

- It appears between 76 and 99% of the duration (median 94%), across 15 films that write it.
- **No product film out of 20 writes it before 76% of its duration.** The name is the payoff, never the title.
- It arrives 2.1 to 5.3 s before the end (median 3.7 s) and stays on screen 3.0 s (median; from 1.3 to 16.3 s).
- 1 to 4 words (median 2).
- In keynotes, the speaker often says the name right after the film (4 product films out of 20). Without a voice, the film **always ends** on the name card (convention).
- A closing line of 3 to 9 words can come before it, in the same place: observed in 3 films; it lasts 2 to 5 s, then the name 2 to 4.5 s.

### 2.5 The end

- After the name: nothing (7 films out of 15), or a logo or black for 0.2 to 3.3 s (8 out of 15).
- The last image shot lasts 5.2 s at the median (19 product films).
- Object films: final logo 0/14, date 0/14.
- Skill rule: the name as the last shot, then a fade to black. The user's logo only if provided (convention).

## 3. Structure scaled to the duration

Bases: median shares from § 2, U-shaped shots (§ 5), 20 cuts per minute for a product film.

| Duration | Hook | Build-up | Reveal (1 held shot) | Name | End | Total shots | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 s | 7–9 s, 2–3 shots of 2.5–4 s | 36–40 s, 15–25 shots of 1–2 s | 6–8 s | 3–4 s | 2–3 s | 20 (15 to 33) | observed: 6 product films from 46.6 to 68.5 s |
| 90 s | 11–13 s, 3–4 shots | 58–62 s, in 2 chapters | 8–9 s | 3.5–4 s | 2–3 s | 30 (23 to 50) | convention |
| 120 s | 14–17 s, 4–5 shots | 83–88 s, in 3 chapters | 9–10 s | 4 s | 3 s | 40 (30 to 66) | convention |

- The shot range goes from the "reveal" pace (19 cuts per minute) to the "software" pace (33). The "use" pace (42) is kept for action edits.
- For a duration between two rows, start from the nearest row and adjust the build-up.
- **No voiceless product film runs past 68.5 s** (observed across 20). So 90 and 120 s are conventions, modeled on voiceless openers (5 films from 65.5 to 114.1 s): chapters separated by black or a card.
- **Recommended default: 60 to 75 s.** Offer 90 or 120 s only if the user has enough to fill 2 or 3 chapters. Rule of thumb (convention): one visual feeds 2 to 3 shots (macro, pull-back, rotation); you need about 7 distinct visuals for 60 s, 10 for 90 s, 14 for 120 s. Otherwise the film repeats itself.

### 3.1 Chapters (90 and 120 s)

Convention, based on voiceless openers and on the reveal module:

- one chapter = **a single idea**: a material, the inside, a use, a lineup, a feature;
- 25 to 35 s, built like a small reveal: from the detail to the whole;
- opened by an idea card (≤ 6 words), or separated from the previous one by 0.5 to 1.5 s of black (10 object films out of 14 go through black at least once);
- the whole object appears only in the last chapter, or comes back wider in each chapter;
- at least one continuous passage of 15 to 40 s with no hard cut in a film of 90 s or more (§ 5.4).

### 3.2 Cards in the structure

Convention, since the observed films have almost none:

- one **thesis card** at the end of the hook (or none);
- one **idea card** per chapter;
- one **feature name** per feature shown, for software (§ 6.3);
- an optional **closing line** before the name;
- the **name**.

### 3.3 The table to show before producing

One row per beat or per chapter. Example for 90 s:

| # | Beat | From → to | What we see | Card | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 0:00 → 0:12 | a glow comes out of black, the material in macro | thesis, 4 words | cut-out photo |
| 2 | Chapter 1: [idea] | 0:12 → 0:42 | 10 short shots, then the whole detail | 1 idea card | macros, parts |
| 3 | Chapter 2: [idea] | 0:42 → 1:14 | continuous passage, 3 features | 3 feature names | screenshots |
| 4 | Reveal | 1:14 → 1:22 | the whole object, slow pull-back | — | cut-out photo |
| 5 | Name and end | 1:22 → 1:30 | closing line, name, black | closing line of 5 words, name | — |

## 4. Film families

The family depends on **the visuals available**, not on preference. With no photo of the object, no levitation: offer an interface film or a text-only film.

| Family | Observed | Median duration (min–max) | Cuts per minute | Observed text | Visuals it needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Object reveal | 8 films | 30.5 s (21.5–68.5) | 19 | 0 explanatory cards out of 8; the name alone in 3 out of 8 | cut-out photos (1 to 5 views), macros, parts |
| Materials and making | 4 films | 80.4 s (46.6–121.5) | 16 | voice-over in 3 out of 4: can't be carried over as is | material macros, parts |
| Software and interface | 6 product films; 12 interface films | 43.5 s (18.9–129.2) | 33 | 0 or 1 card in the 5 voiceless interface films | screenshots, screen recordings |
| Use and advertising | 2 films | 77.2 s (60.4–93.9) | 42 | one sentence then the name, 2 out of 2 | mood photos or videos the user has rights to |
| Text-only film | 1 film (the only one in the corpus made of text and music) | 71 s | — | text on screen 85% of the time, about 21 fragments per minute | none: type and colors |

When to pick what (convention):

- **Physical object** with cut-out photos: object reveal. The material can make a chapter (macro, streak of light), with no voice.
- **Software, app, online service** with screenshots: software and interface.
- **Both** (an object with a screen): reveal structure, with the interface in the build-up.
- **People in context**, with images the user has rights to: use. Faster edit.
- **Nothing to show**, or a message more than an object: text-only film. Tell the user, and give them the list of visuals that would allow a better film.

The text-only film, as observed (1 film, 71 s, music only):

- three sections, each on a different flat background (black, light gray with black text, black);
- one fragment of 2 to 8 words at a time, on one line, centered, small (4.4 to 4.8% of the height);
- word by word: each word arrives in gray, then turns solid in 0.25 to 0.5 s, and the line re-centers;
- the full fragment holds 0.75 to 1.5 s, fades out over 0.3 to 0.5 s, then 0.5 to 1 s of black;
- about 1 word per second: the viewer has time to feel the sentence;
- three or four times, the word does what it says (it grows, its letters scatter and come back together).

## 5. Rhythm

### 5.1 Shot length by beat

Product films and openers, reliable shots.

| Beat | Shots measured | Median | 1st quartile | 3rd quartile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 56 | 2.80 s | 1.16 s | 4.29 s |
| Build-up | 662 | 1.17 s | 0.67 s | 2.10 s |
| Action peak before the end | 51 | 1.40 s | 0.95 s | 1.94 s |
| Reveal | 17 | 5.54 s | 2.74 s | 8.29 s |
| Name | 15 | 2.54 s | 0.91 s | 4.07 s |

### 5.2 The U shape

- Long shots at the start, short in the middle, very long at the end: observed in 13 product films out of 17.
- **No speed-up to the end.** Product films: 7 speed up in the second half, 5 slow down, 5 stay steady (17 films). They slow down mainly for the reveal.
- Edits, on the other hand, speed up: 3 interface edits out of 4 have a faster last third, and 4 trailers out of 5. Allowed in the build-up of a use film, never for the reveal (convention).
- At least one shot of 5 s or more in the film: observed 16/19. The longest shot lasts 8.3 s at the median (19 films).

### 5.3 Cuts per minute

| Family | Cuts per minute (median) |
|---|---|
| Reveal | 19 |
| Materials | 16 |
| Software | 33 |
| Use, advertising | 42 |
| Trailer (reference) | 43 |

The median shot stays between 0.9 and 2.3 s in every family that cuts. The difference comes from the long shots.

### 5.4 Continuous motion

- A continuous camera move or a transformation counts as one shot.
- 5 interface films out of 12 hold a passage of 20 to 52 s with no hard cut; one object film holds a shot of 26.7 s.
- Convention: at least one continuous passage of 15 to 40 s in a film of 90 s or more. The engine has no long take: chain 3 to 5 shots with `transition: "fondu"` (crossfade), with a match on shape or content from one shot to the next. Keep the crossfade for this passage; elsewhere the hard cut stays the rule (14/14).

### 5.5 Blacks

- 100 blacks measured: median 0.65 s (quartiles 0.32 and 2.0 s).
- 10 object films out of 14 go through black at least once, held 0.5 to 1.5 s.
- Black works as punctuation: between two chapters, before the reveal, after the name.

## 6. Text without a voice

### 6.1 What the films do

- Text is rare. Across 18 product films: 8 with no added text at all, 1 with a simple bar, 6 with only the name at the end, 3 with **one single sentence** (3, 6 or 9 words). No product film has more than one sentence.
- No number card in any film: 0 out of 48. The big number is a slide device.
- In the frames reviewed, never more than one block of added text at a time (12 interface films).

### 6.2 How much text

| Case | Cards per minute | Share of time with text | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Image films, with a speaker around them | 1 to 3 | 4 to 8% | observed (6 films measured) |
| Text-only film, music only | about 21 | about 85% | observed (1 film) |
| **Our product film, 60 to 120 s** | **6 to 10**, all texts counted (cards, feature names, closing line, name) | **30 to 45%** | convention (checked by `check_film.py`) |

At most 45 words per minute (convention). The pattern: **a card, then the image that proves it.** It is observed at the end of a documentary film: image of 2 to 8 s, card of 1.25 to 2.25 s, image…

### 6.3 Card types

Sizes for a 1920 × 1080 frame. "Size" = font size in px.

| Type | Words | Duration | Size | Placement | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis | 3 to 6 | 3 to 4 s | 50 to 64 px | center, its own shot on a flat background | observed at the start of 4 films out of 11 |
| Idea card (chapter) | 6 at most | rule in § 6.4 | 52 to 64 px | center, its own shot | convention |
| Feature name, next to a screen | 1 to 4, 2 lines at most | the whole shot, 4 to 6 s | about 68 px, semibold | right of the screen, centered at 81% of the width | observed in 2 presentations (not films) |
| Punch word | 1 or 2 | 1 to 2 s | 130 to 170 px, bold | center | observed 5 times, in trailers |
| **Provided** number | the number + a label of 6 words at most | rule in § 6.4 | number 130 to 170 px | center, label below | 0/48 in films; taken from slides |
| **Provided** quote | 12 at most | 4 to 6 s | the card's size | observed: in an empty area of the image, left-aligned; in the engine: a `carton`, centered | observed 1 film (review) |
| Closing line | 3 to 9 | 2 to 5 s | 52 to 64 px | where the following name sits | observed 3 films |
| Name | 1 to 4 (median 2) | 2.5 to 5 s (median 3.0) | discreet 44 to 64 px; hero 110 to 150 px | center | observed 15 films |

### 6.4 Length and duration

- **6 words at most** for a card that appears all at once: observed in 11 sentences out of 14.
- From 7 to 12 words, the card is **built** in 2 or 3 meaning groups (a clause, then its payoff).
- **Never more than 12 words**: observed in 106 cards out of 109; the longest sentence seen has 11 words. A longer sentence splits into two cards, at a meaning boundary.
- Per line, at most 42 characters in 16:9, 22 in 9:16, 26 in 1:1 (convention: leaves room for languages that run longer than the English of the corpus).
- **Minimum time on screen: 1.2 s + 0.3 s per word**; at least 1.5 s for a sentence, 1.0 s for a lone punch word, 2.5 s for the name. Below that, `check_film.py` blocks (convention tuned to what was observed). 2 words: 1.8 s; 4 words: 2.4 s; 6 words: 3.0 s; 9 words: 3.9 s; 12 words: 4.8 s. This rule passes through the observed medians (6 words ≈ 3.0 s; 9 words ≈ 4.0 s; 11 words ≈ 4.75 s) and stays above the fastest cards in the corpus (3 to 4 words per second), which lived next to a voice.
- **Recommended duration: 1.5 s + 0.3 s per word, never under 2 s.** Below that, the checker warns. It sits between the observed median and 3rd quartile, because here the card is the only source of meaning. Films without speech even allow 1 to 1.5 words per second: in a slow film, hold longer.
- Observed references: 2.0 s for 2 to 5 words, 3.0 s for 6 to 8 words, 4.0 s for 9 to 12 words (medians, 109 cards). A card of 9 words or more never stays under 2.5 s (20 out of 20).
- The shot carrying the card adds about 0.6 s for entry and exit: a 5-word card needs a shot of at least 3.3 s, 3.6 s recommended.
- Built card: duration counts from the last group to appear. The full sentence holds at least 1.0 s + 0.1 s per word (convention; observed 0.75 to 2.3 s).

### 6.5 Size, placement, type

- **Small and calm.** The message is 3.7 to 6.1% of the frame height (10 cards measured out of 10, median 5%, so 52 to 64 px at 1080). That is the size of a cinema subtitle. The strength comes from the empty space around it.
- Line width: 12 to 66% of the frame width (9 lines measured). Never more than 66%.
- **Centered on both axes**: observed 17/17 clean measurements. Exceptions seen 5 times out of 33: under the object, in an empty area of the image, bottom left for a person's name.
- One sans-serif type family: observed 32/33. It comes from the user's branding.
- Regular or medium weight for sentences (13/14); bold for the hero name and punch words (5/5).
- Sentence case (13/14). No all caps for a sentence, no italics, no exclamation mark (0/14).
- Monochrome: light on dark, or dark on light. Color only on the name (3/33). The accent comes from the user's HEX values.
- No box, no bar, no plate behind the text: 0/33.

### 6.6 Text motion

| Device | Observed | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Short fade-in | at least 10 cases | 0.25 to 0.5 s; the text goes from gray to solid |
| Hard cut (the card is a shot) | about 9 cases | appears and disappears on a cut |
| Word by word | 4 cases | 0.3 to 0.5 s per word; the line re-centers at each word (4/4) |
| Group by group | 4 cases | 0.6 to 2.5 s between two groups |
| Slow fade (name) | 2 cases | 1.0 to 1.5 s |
| The image arrives under the text, which stays | 1 case | the text is read before the image |
| The word does what it says | 5 cases in 3 films | **one per film**, two at most in 2 min (convention) |

- Each new word or group arrives in gray (about 40% opacity), then turns solid in 0.25 to 0.33 s: observed 5/5.
- Exit: cut, fade of 0.3 to 0.5 s, or dissolve into the next shot.
- One way of animating sentences per film; the name may have another (convention).
- Animate only opacity, position and scale. Not letter spacing, weight or text blur: the line would jump (convention).
- **Never seen (0/33)**: bounce, "pop" effect, slide in from an edge, letter-by-letter typewriter, karaoke-style highlight, shake, heavy drop shadow. The skill forbids them.

### 6.7 Text and image

- **The card is its own shot**, on a flat background: 23 texts out of 33. This is the base rule. On flat black: 19 out of 33.
- On an image: only in a calm area (sky, night, haze, blur, a shot that darkens before the text), or in the empty part of the composition. Contrast of at least 7:1 on an image (convention), 4.5:1 everywhere (checked by `npx hyperframes check`). Two texts in the corpus drop to 3:1: don't copy them.
- **The text does not describe the image.** It states the idea; the image is the proof (observed: no card says what we see). Test: without the image, the card keeps its meaning; without the card, the image does not say the same thing.
- **Never two cards on screen at the same time** (observed: never more than one block at a time).
- Between two message cards, at least one shot with no text, of 1.5 s or more, except in a text-only film sequence (convention).
- At least 6 s with no text per minute: a passage of images only (convention).
- A break in rhythm about every 30 s: a lone word, a background change, a passage with no text (convention; observed in the text-only film: 3 sections on 3 backgrounds).

### 6.8 Numbers, quotes, reviews

- **No invented numbers.** A number card only uses a number provided by the user. Otherwise it shows `[TO PROVIDE]` and stays a visible gap in the storyboard.
- The label under the number includes the basis of comparison ("compared with …") when there is one.
- Numbers already visible in the user's screenshots are theirs: they can be shown, never invented or changed.
- Quotes and reviews: only those provided, with the author and their consent. Stars only if the real rating is provided.
- A wording proposed by the skill (flagged "TO VALIDATE" in `notes`) contains no number, not even spelled out.

## 7. The name and the end

- **Two sizes.** Discreet: 4 to 5% of the height (44 to 64 px), observed in 2 object films out of 2 that write the name; the object stays the hero. Hero: 9 to 13% of the height (110 to 150 px), observed in 2 interface films out of 2; for software or a service, where there is no object. The object / software split is a convention.
- Alone, centered, on the film's background. Neutral color (2/2 object films); the user's accent or gradient is allowed on the name only (3/33).
- Entry: cut, or fade of 0.3 to 0.5 s, **with no motion**. A slow fade of 1 to 1.5 s is allowed for a name coming out of shadow (2 cases). From white, 2 to 2.5 s (1 case).
- No date and no slogan under the name: 0/14 object films. A call-to-action line (address, release date) only if provided; it is mainly for the cut-downs (convention).
- Then black: the engine closes the long film with a 0.6 s fade to black (within the observed range of 0.2 to 3.3 s). The cut-downs have no final black.

## 8. The image: camera, light, color, transitions

The recipes in `recettes.md` reproduce these devices in animation, with no filming and no 3D.

### 8.1 Camera (14 object films)

| Device | Observed | Reference setting |
|---|---|---|
| Macro of a material or detail | 12/14 | shot of 1.5 to 3 s, slow drift |
| Levitation (the object floats) | 9/14 | soft shadow or reflection; never a visible stand |
| Slow rotation | 8/14 | 30 to 90° per shot; never a full turn |
| Assembly (the parts close together) | 7/14 | no visible bounce |
| Hands holding it | 7/14 | excluded without photos the user has rights to |
| Exploded view (parts separated) | 6/14 | vertical stack or radial |
| Reveal pull-back | 6/14 | size × 0.4 to 0.65 over 5 to 7 s, easing out |
| Marked push-in | 5/14 | toward a detail |
| Top-down view | 4/14 | |
| The image was the screen (a pull-back reveals that a full-frame image is the object's screen) | 3/14 | |

### 8.2 Screens (12 interface films)

| Device | Observed |
|---|---|
| Interface with no device outline: full frame, floating panel, or isolated element | 7/12 |
| The camera moves into the screen, toward a detail | 6/12 |
| The rest blurred (shallow depth of field) | 6/12 |
| The isolated element on a flat background | 4/12 |
| Added arrow, circle, frame or callout box | **0/12** |
| Visible cursor | 4/12, never more than 2 shots per film |
| Click circle or ripple | 0/12 |

Isolate, don't annotate. Move into the screen, don't point at it.

### 8.3 Light

| Device | Observed (14 object films) |
|---|---|
| A streak of light sweeping a surface, one pass of 2.5 to 4 s | 4/14 |
| Light switch (the set goes dark or lights up within the shot) | 4/14 |
| Rim light: the object drawn only by its lit outline, on black | 3/14 |
| Colored glow behind or under the object | 3/14 |
| Backlight, black silhouettes on a very light background | 3/14 |
| Point of light, star glint, often before black | 3/14 |

Never seen: hard front light, sharp black shadow, a set lit brighter than the object.

### 8.4 Color and backgrounds

- Light background in 7 object films out of 14, dark in 5, natural setting in 2. Never a busy set behind the object alone.
- **White backgrounds are never pure white**: 0/7 light films. Measured references: #F0F4F3 to #F6F6F8 (off-white), #D2D4D3 to #DEDCDF (light gray). Dark backgrounds are often charcoal with a gradient toward the top, not a flat fill.
- **One strong color per film**, taken from the product or from a light: 7/14. Strict monochrome: 2/14.
- Interfaces: very light gray background (#E0E2E6 to #F8F8FA, 3 films) or black (5 films). The color comes from the interface itself.
- In the skill (convention): light background = the user's light neutral, dark background = their dark neutral, one accent color per film.

### 8.5 Transitions (14 object films)

| Transition | Observed |
|---|---|
| Hard cut | 14/14 (the default) |
| Through black, 0.5 to 1.5 s | 10/14 |
| Shape match (the end shape of one shot is the start of the next) | 7/14 |
| Light switch | 4/14 |
| Continuous motion that replaces the cut | 1/14; 5/12 interface films |
| Whip (the object leaves the frame in a blur) | 1/14 |
| Crossfade | can't be verified at 2 frames per second: keep it for a continuous passage (§ 5.4) (convention) |

No abrupt camera move (0/12), no bounce (0/14).

### 8.6 What the skill does not promise

- A true 360° turn from a single photo: rotation of ± 12° at most.
- True slow motion (liquid, falling): it is filmed in the corpus, and the engine has no recipe to imitate it. Do without.
- A 3D fly-over close to the ground: no fly-over and no parallax in the engine.
- The inside of an object that the user does not provide: no invented exploded view.
- Hands, people, silhouettes, without photos the user has rights to.

## 9. Music

- **Skill rule: music is a file provided by the user, who holds the rights to it, or there is none.** Never music that is downloaded, generated or taken from a library. The HyperFrames skill `product-launch-video` fetches music online by default: don't follow that path.
- **Cutting on the beat: not demonstrated.** Across 6 films and 62 cuts, 16 land within ± 70 ms of a strong attack, against 16.4 expected by chance. Two interface films give the same result. Reveal films follow the music through continuous motion and big changes (black, flash, arrival of the name), not by cutting on each beat.
- With music (convention): align shot starts with strong beats (`musique.caler_coupes: true`; the engine reads the given grid, or the one from `npx hyperframes beats`); land the name on an accent; end on a resolution; 2 s fade-out.
- Without music (convention): regular shots of 2 to 3 s, 0.5 s blacks as breathing room. The film stays readable: it has no voice, so it works with the sound off.

## 10. What we never take

The grammar, never the brand. Even "in the style of":

- no image, no sound, no excerpt from the keynotes;
