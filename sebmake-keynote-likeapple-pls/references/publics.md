# Audiences: which devices to keep, which to adapt

> The skill asks a single question about the audience, then applies the matching row. This file says, for each audience, what the skill keeps from the corpus devices and what it adapts.
> The register given here is only a suggestion: in French, tu or vous is **always asked** of the user (see `grammaire.md` §7.1); in English, the casual or formal tone is asked.

**Starting point.** The corpus is made of general-public keynotes from a very well-known brand. Its setting: no sources under the figures, charts without axis ticks, anonymous competitors in product launches, many “up to” claims, bounded superlatives (“yet”). That is a setting for the general public, not a universal one. In front of other audiences, it loses trust.

## 1. The question to ask

“Who is the presentation for?” A single question, with these choices:

1. B2B clients: decision makers (leadership, business)
2. B2B clients: buyers (purchasing, tender)
3. End customers, general public
4. Investors
5. Partners and resellers
6. Internal team: sprint review, product demo or all-hands
7. Trade show or conference
8. Recruiting
9. I don't know, or a mixed audience

For the internal team, the skill infers the sub-format from the topic. It asks only if it stays unclear. For a mixed audience, it asks which audiences are present, then applies §5.

## 2. The devices at stake and their levels

| Device | Levels, from the most free to the most careful |
|---|---|
| **Sources under the figures** | *No*: everything on the final card, like the corpus. *Discreet*: a numbered note (¹) under the figure, the full source on the final card. *Yes*: the source readable under the figure, in gray, at 3 % of H at least. |
| **Charts** | *No axis ticks*: only the gap is written, with its base (corpus). *Values written*: no axis, but both values at the end of the bars. *Scaled*: axes, units, dates, origin at zero for bars. |
| **Competitors** | *Named*: only in an objective, sourced comparison. *Anonymous*: a category (“the solutions on the market”, “the manual way”, “the spreadsheet”). *Absent*. |
| **“up to”** | *With condition*: the condition is written under the figure or as a note. *Typical value first*: the common case on screen; the maximum only aloud, with its condition. *Banned*: only real measurements are shown. |
| **Price** | *Tax-included “from”* (A19), *before tax per unit and per period*, *business model*, *on quote*, *none*. Always provided by the user, otherwise `[TO PROVIDE: price]`. |
| **Superlatives** | *N1 relative to yourself* (“our most [adj] [X] yet”): true against your own versions. *N2 a first* (“for the first time”): must be true. *N3 absolute* (“the best on the market”, “in the world”): requires cited external proof. |
| **Demo** | Its place in the sequence (step 10) and its form: live, filmed, or acted out by people. |

## 4. The audience cards

### 4.1 B2B clients (decision makers, buyers)

- **What they want.** The decision maker: the gain, the risk, the timeline. The buyer: the price, the contract, compliance, integration.
- **Keep.** The announcement sequence, the demo, the mini-recap, the thesis sentence (A4) that states the gain.
- **Adapt.** The tension becomes the cost of the current situation, quantified by the client if they provided it. The safeguard (step 13: security, data, compliance) gains weight and can have its own block. Third-party proof goes through comparable clients, with their agreement.
- **Drop.** The long opening video, the aside, absolute superlatives.
- **Trap.** Putting on a show where the buyer expects answers. Put the details (price grid, terms, technical appendices) in a separate document, announced at the end.

### 4.2 End customers, general public

- **What they want.** What it changes in their day, how much, when.
- **Keep.** Almost all of the corpus: video before the name, name alone on screen, giant figure, anchored price, availability date, bento.
- **Adapt.** Each technical figure gets its concrete equivalent (“enough to [X]”). The conditions of “up to” claims go in a note, detailed on the final card.
- **Drop.** Jargon, technical diagrams (A11, A12) except for an enthusiast audience.
- **Trap.** Stacking figures. One or two per announcement are enough; the rest goes through the demo.

### 4.3 Investors

- **What they want.** The problem, the market size, the traction, the business model, the team, the use of funds.
- **Keep.** The rhythm, one idea per screen, the giant figure, the final recap in three terms, the same person opening and closing.
- **Adapt.** Each figure carries its source and its date. The forecast is marked as such, with its assumptions. The competitive landscape names the players. The announcement sequence serves the product only; the rest follows the order problem, solution, market, traction, model, team, ask.
- **Drop.** Charts with no axis, “up to” claims, superlatives, the opening video (or 15 s at most).
- **Trap.** A curve with no axis or a projection with no assumption: trust drops for everything that follows.

### 4.4 Partners and resellers

- **What they want.** What they gain, the effort asked, the support provided.
- **Keep.** The announcement sequence for the product, then a second sequence for the partner offer (program name, benefits, terms, date).
- **Adapt.** The demo shows their journey: integrate, sell, get help. The quantified terms go in a separate document.
- **Drop.** Comparisons with named competitors: some partners also work with them.
- **Trap.** Talking about the product the whole time and forgetting what the partner gains.

### 4.5 Internal team (sprint review, product demo, all-hands)

- **What they want.** What is done, what works, what is coming, and who did it.
- **Keep.** The handovers (each person presents what they did), the mini-recap, the nested recaps, the same person opening and closing.
- **Adapt.** *Sprint review*: one block per goal; each finished item has its name, its demo, then its exact status (in production, in testing, in progress). *Product demo*: the demo takes most of the time. *All-hands*: the full run of show, with a possible aside on a team success.
- **Drop.** The spectacular opening video, superlatives, the launch tone.
- **Trap.** Announcing as shipped what is not, in front of people who know what goes on behind the scenes. And forgetting to name the people who did the work.

### 4.6 Trade show or conference

- **What it wants.** One strong idea, remembered on the way out of the room.
- **Keep.** The short video, the name alone, the giant figure, a filmed demo, a clean recap.
- **Adapt.** The time is set by the organizer: aim for the slot minus 10 %. The demo is filmed by default; live only if it works without the venue's network. Follow the organizer's rules (often: no direct selling in a conference talk).
- **Drop.** Named competitors, detailed pricing outside a launch.
- **Trap.** Running over the slot, or depending on the venue's network for the demo.

### 4.7 Recruiting

- **What it wants.** The job, the team, the product, the way of working, the next steps of the process.
- **Keep.** The rhythm, the handovers (several voices from the team), the product demo, the final recap.
- **Adapt.** Third-party proof becomes the words of team members, real and agreed to. Figures about the company carry their source and date.
- **Drop.** Superlatives about the culture, competitors, prices.
- **Trap.** A slide of generic values and promises nobody can check. Show a real day instead.

## 5. The default rule: unknown or mixed audience

- **Unknown audience**: apply the “Default” row of the table. It is careful: visible sources under each figure, scaled charts or values written, anonymous competitors, “up to” only with its condition next to it, no superlative that can't be checked (N1 only if true, never N3), price only if provided.
- **Mixed audience**: for each device, take the most careful setting of the audiences present. Example: clients and investors together, so visible sources and scaled charts.
- In both cases, the skill says so in its notes to the user, in one sentence, so they can relax a setting.

## 6. Legal safeguards (general rule)

These are not legal advice. They are reflexes, valid in France and the European Union.

- **Comparing with a named competitor** is allowed only when comparing offers that meet the same need, on essential, objective and verifiable characteristics. When in doubt: make it anonymous.
- **A superlative or a figure that can't be checked** can be judged misleading. An “up to” must be reachable under the stated conditions.
- **A logo, a testimonial, a client photo** need the owner's agreement.
- **In front of investors**, a forecast is presented as a forecast, with its assumptions.
- **A price shown to the general public** includes tax; a B2B price states that it is before tax.
- When a setting stays doubtful, the skill picks the most careful one and tells the user.
